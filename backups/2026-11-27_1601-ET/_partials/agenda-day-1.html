    Corruption and human rights abuses reinforce each other. The misuse of entrusted power for personal gain benefits a few while harming the most vulnerable in…Corruption and human rights abuses reinforce each other. The misuse of entrusted power for personal gain benefits a few while harming the most vulnerable in society. Simultaneously, human rights violations create conditions where corruption can thrive. A human rights-based approach to anti-corruption action helps enhance accountability, reveal abuses of power, empower affected communities, and rebuild trust in institutions. It mobilises governments, donors, courts, and civil society to achieve tangible, people-centred integrity outcomes. An increasing number of innovative approaches aim to integrate both agendas and ensure progress on rights, justice, and democratic accountability. Across regions, national institutions are beginning to implement new approaches. Courts are recognising corruption as a human rights harm and expanding victims’ standing. National human rights institutions (NHRIs) are engaging more directly with anti-corruption bodies. Executive agencies are linking integrity strategies, asset recovery, and public ethics frameworks to human rights outcomes. These experiences offer valuable lessons on what works, what does not, and how institutions can cooperate more effectively. At the 22nd International Anti-Corruption Conference (IACC), the United Nations Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR), the Bundesministerium für wirtschaftliche Zusammenarbeit und Entwicklung (BMZ)/Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit GmbH (GIZ), the U4 Anti-Corruption Resource Centre, and the Global Civil Society Coalition for the UNCAC are joining efforts to organise a session focused on innovative tools and alliances for human rights–based anti-corruption action. The workshop will provide a safe, interactive space for dialogue on integrating human rights into anti-corruption practices, drawing on innovative experiences from governments, donors, courts, and civil society. It aims to be acaction anded, connecting committed actors, laying the groundwork for a group of like-minded partners interested on supporting human rights–based anti-corruption action, and fostering discussions on the issue at the next Conference of States Parties (CoSP) and its subsidiary bodies..     Created as a collective work with Lorena and her husband as co-directors,”Querida Fátima” embodies radical solidarity, placing the power of non-fiction cinema in the hands of those who have lived the story and using it as a compelling tool to uplift their voices.
